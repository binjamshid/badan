# BADAN data and privacy

This overview follows BADAN revision `8b83fed` for iOS 1.2 and Android 0.1.0. It describes what the apps ask for, keep locally, share through operating system features, and send through optional services. You can read the policy bundled in your iOS app under **Settings → Privacy & security → Privacy Policy**. [iOS policy draft](PRIVACY_POLICY.md) · [Bilingual policy draft](legal/privacy.html) · [User guide](USER_GUIDE.md)

## What BADAN reads

| Data | iOS | Android |
|---|---|---|
| System health source | BADAN requests read access to Apple Health. The requested families cover sleep and stages, heart and respiratory readings, activity and energy, cardio fitness, body composition, blood glucose, and workouts. The request includes height and sleeping wrist temperature without an ingest mapping for those two measurements. | BADAN reads supported types from Health Connect. They include activity and exercise, sleep, heart and respiratory readings, cardio fitness, skin temperature, body composition, and blood glucose. The operating system controls access by type. |
| Workout routes | A separate BADAN consent and Apple Health access govern workout-route reads. User-selected imports can also contain routes. | Workout-route reads require separate BADAN consent and exercise access. Health Connect can require a per-session grant. |
| Records you choose to add | Manual entries include workouts, nutrition, hydration and notes. Optional sensitive modules include medications, symptoms, journals, lab documents, cycles and pregnancy. Lab input can use the camera, a selected photo or a selected PDF. Recognition runs on the device. | Manual entries include workouts, nutrition, hydration, journals, medications and symptoms. The manifest declares permissions for future capabilities, including camera access. Android lab document capture is unavailable in this revision. |
| Background and device features | Apple Health updates can trigger background reads after connection. Notifications and display surfaces have their own settings. | Background Health Connect reading is optional and depends on system support and permission. Notification and activity permissions are separate from access to health records. |

Using **Explore with sample data** generates local sample readings. **Start empty** gives you an empty setup for manual entry. BADAN's connect and import actions copy compatible records into its own store. ## Local storage

Both apps keep ordinary and sensitive records in separate SQLite databases. iOS encrypts the sensitive database with SQLCipher and keeps its key in the device Keychain. iOS requests file protection and backup exclusion for the health database directory. Those settings are best-effort. Android uses standard SQLite for both databases in this revision, with no separate BADAN database encryption. The Android manifest disables app backup. Operating system handling, including device transfer, depends on the platform and device. [iOS storage](../apps/ios/Sources/Storage/Database.swift) · [Android storage](../apps/android/app/src/main/java/net/albayyan/badan/storage/Database.kt) · [Android manifest](../apps/android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)

Turning off **Meds & symptoms** after confirming deletes the sensitive store used by the switch. In the iOS build that store can also contain other sensitive modules and documents. Check and export the relevant records before confirming. Cycle, pregnancy and other sensitive features on iOS have their own controls as well.

On iOS, your display choices can expose a small summary on widgets and a paired Apple Watch. The widget and watch cache writers in this revision do not set an explicit backup exclusion. Check **Settings → Privacy & security** for display controls, including the separate medication reminder setting for widgets. Workout route previews can be drawn locally. If you choose **Show on map** on iOS, Apple Maps supplies the map background and may use a network connection. Android draws its route preview from local points. ## Exports and backups

BADAN assembles CSV and JSON exports on the device, with a manifest for checking the files. **Quick Export** omits medication and symptom records and route data. Advanced export has separate choices for including supported sensitive data and workout routes. Sensitive exports require passphrase encryption. Ordinary CSV and JSON exports can contain private health information in plain text.

Choosing **Share** or saving an export makes the file available at the destination you choose. A cloud-backed folder, mail app or other recipient can keep its own copy. Scheduled exports or shortcuts can also write to a folder you selected. Direct export from BADAN to a REST server is disabled in the included builds. You can preview a supported BADAN backup on both platforms. Previewing reads the file locally and currently has no apply control. [Export bundle format](../packages/export-schemas/src/export_bundle.md) · [Export and backup steps](USER_GUIDE.md#export-or-preview-a-backup)

## Optional services

These iOS features depend on Apple services and a compatible BADAN service deployment. Health ingestion, local scores, logs and export creation work without a BADAN account. The Android source has no connected BADAN account, coach, or methodology submission flow. Its handset manifest does not declare Internet access. Packaged permissions depend on the merged manifest, including declarations from dependencies. Sharing a file and opening the Health Connect store listing hand off to another app.

| iOS feature | What is sent or stored | Your control |
|---|---|---|
| **AI coach** | Enabling it uses Apple App Attest and creates an installation token. The service stores a token hash and attestation metadata. Each send includes your current question, earlier questions and replies from the open chat, and the available Readiness, Effort and Sleep summary for today, with confidence, method and date when available. BADAN adds derived context from the main database and does not automatically include raw records. The BADAN service forwards the request through OpenRouter to a hosted model. Its source implements no persistence for chat content. The hosted provider processes the request under its own policies. | Start from **Log → AI coach**. **Context included** shows the score summary on the sent message. Avoid putting personal identifiers or private records in the chat. Turning the coach off clears the local token and attempts to revoke it with the service. A request already sent may finish processing. |
| **Methodology feedback** | Optional submissions summarize your responses about whether a calculated result matches your experience. A metric needs at least ten responses before a summary can be sent. The payload includes the metric and method version, direction, count range, whether calibration was applied, and app and contract versions. The request includes an App Attest key identifier and assertion, and the service uses a network address for rate limiting. Stored feedback rows include the receipt day and omit the attestation key identifier, account or device identifiers, health readings and reading dates. | The setting starts off. Go to **Settings → Privacy & security → Send methodology feedback** to inspect the submission log or turn sharing off. Stored feedback rows lack a per-user deletion key. |
| **Sign in with Apple and legacy purchases** | Optional sign-in sends an Apple identity token to the BADAN account service. BADAN does not request your Apple name or email. The account stores an account ID, a keyed hash of the app-specific Apple identifier, creation date, referral details and any purchase status previously reported by older releases. It holds no health history. The current app sends no new purchase status. | In **Settings → Account**, sign out to clear the session on this device, or use **Delete account** to remove the server account. Manage App Store subscriptions through Apple. |

The repository implementation keeps feedback payloads separate from attestation records and logs request metadata such as status and timing. Server configuration, service availability, and hosted-provider retention require separate verification for any distributed build. ## Revoke access or delete data

- **Revoke Apple Health or Health Connect access** in the corresponding system app to stop future reads. Copies already imported into BADAN can still be present. On iOS, the **Read workout routes** toggle in **Data sources → Workout routes** also stops future route reads. On Android, you can revoke exercise access in Health Connect after leaving the in-app consent screen.
- **Delete all data** clears BADAN’s local databases. On iOS it keeps app preferences and attempts to re-import from Apple Health if connected or populate the demo otherwise. On Android it attempts to load the demo even when Health Connect is connected and can reset customization preferences. System health records and earlier exports are managed separately. Revoke the app’s access at the system level to stop later imports.
- **Delete account** on iOS removes the optional server account and its referral code. Your local health records and Apple subscription are managed separately. Turning off the coach or methodology sharing uses its own controls.

The repository iOS policy draft follows the current source. Device behavior, store disclosures and hosted policy updates are separate release checks.
