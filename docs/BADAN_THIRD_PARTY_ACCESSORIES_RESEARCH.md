# BADAN Third-Party Accessories and Device Ecosystem Research

## 1. Executive framing

BADAN's ambition to become a durable, user-owned personal health repository depends on its ability to ingest data from the sprawling and fragmented world of consumer health hardware — smartwatches, rings, straps, scales, cuffs, and fitness machines — without becoming hostage to any single vendor, without silently corrupting data quality by merging incompatible sources, and without ever compromising the privacy of what is, after period-tracking and pregnancy data, one of the most sensitive categories of personal information a person can generate about their body. This document is a pre-engineering research and planning foundation, not a specification. It maps the practical landscape of platform, vendor, and direct-device integration paths as they exist today, translates BADAN's seven product laws into concrete architectural and UX commitments, and produces implementation-ready artifacts — data models, decision matrices, screen inventories, edge-case catalogs, and a phased backlog — for a subsequent execution-planning phase.

The central engineering reality this document confronts is that no single integration mechanism covers the device landscape. HealthKit and Health Connect are the most reliable, lowest-friction, and most privacy-respecting paths where a device or its companion app already writes into them, but neither platform is a universal solution: Health Connect explicitly does not carry ECG data or many vendor-proprietary metrics [web:16], and vendor support is uneven and slowly evolving (Garmin only added Health Connect support in mid-2025, and even then as a one-way, partial data flow excluding running power, detailed cadence, and possibly SpO2/HRV/body temperature) [web:55][web:61]. Below the platform layer sits a long tail of vendor APIs (Garmin Connect's Health API, Dexcom's developer API), direct Bluetooth Low Energy standard profiles (Heart Rate Service, Cycling Power Service, and similar GATT-defined services) [web:48][web:51][web:52], and — increasingly — Apple's AccessorySetupKit, a privacy-preserving pairing framework introduced in iOS 18 that lets an app pair with a specific BLE accessory without requesting broad nearby-device Bluetooth permission [web:34][web:35][web:38][web:42][web:43]. BADAN's strategy must choose, for every device class, the lowest-friction and most privacy-respecting route that is realistically available, and must be explicit with users about the tier of integration actually in use.

## 2. Product thesis

BADAN's device ecosystem exists to make the "one data model, two altitudes" law concrete in the domain where it is hardest to hold: hardware and vendor fragmentation. A casual user should be able to tap "connect Apple Health" or "connect Health Connect," grant permission once, and see meaningful summaries appear without ever learning the words "GATT," "OAuth scope," or "sync cursor." A power user — the kind of user BADAN's own design philosophy assumes exists in meaningful numbers — should be able to open a Device Hub and see every connected source, every permission granted, every sync timestamp, every raw sample, every reconciliation decision BADAN made when two devices disagreed, and export all of it. Both experiences must be rendered from the same canonical data model; there is no "lite" schema and no "pro" schema, only different presentation policies over identical underlying records.

Provenance is the mechanism that makes this thesis trustworthy rather than merely convenient. Every measurement BADAN stores must be traceable to the device, connection type, and timestamp that produced it, because health data without provenance is not a repository — it is an unverifiable guess. This matters most exactly where it is hardest: when two devices disagree about the same fact (a chest strap and a watch reporting different heart rates during the same ten minutes), BADAN's obligation under "honest interpretation" is to say so plainly rather than quietly averaging the disagreement away.

## 4. Jobs to be done

- "When I get a new watch, ring, or scale, let me connect it in under a minute without reading documentation."
- "When two of my devices disagree about the same workout or the same night's sleep, tell me plainly rather than pretending they agree."
- "Let me see, at a glance, every device and app that has ever contributed data to my BADAN record."
- "Let me export everything a specific device has ever contributed, or everything overall, without asking anyone's permission but my own."
- "Never let a subscription lapse cut me off from data I already own."
- "Tell me clearly when BADAN cannot integrate directly with a device, and give me a real alternative (import, manual entry) rather than a dead end."
- "Never make a consumer device's reading feel like a medical verdict."
- "Let me revoke a connection and delete its data cleanly, without residue."
- "Support my language and right-to-left layout as a first-class citizen, not an afterthought."

## 5. Device ecosystem strategy

BADAN's overarching ecosystem strategy rests on four sequenced priorities. First, treat HealthKit and Health Connect as the default, lowest-maintenance-cost ingestion layer for any device whose vendor already writes into them — this covers the largest practical share of mainstream smartwatches, rings, and scales with the least ongoing engineering burden, since Apple and Google absorb the vendor-integration work themselves [web:16][web:36]. Second, build a small number of high-value direct vendor integrations (OAuth-based APIs) only where platform-mediated access is insufficient — either because the vendor doesn't write into HealthKit/Health Connect at all, writes only a subset of its data, or where BADAN needs functionality the platform doesn't expose (e.g., historical backfill depth, or richer metadata). Third, build a general-purpose, standards-based BLE ingestion framework covering the well-defined GATT service categories (Heart Rate, Cycling Power, Cycling Speed and Cadence, Weight Scale, Blood Pressure, Health Thermometer, Pulse Oximeter, Fitness Machine) so that BADAN gains broad compatibility with the long tail of BLE-only sensors and machines without vendor-specific work for each one [web:48][web:49][web:51][web:52]. Fourth, provide dignified, transparent fallback for everything else: file import, document import, and manual logging, always paired with an honest "we don't support direct integration with X yet" message rather than silence.

## 7. Competitive benchmark and market gaps

Published accounts of the health-app device landscape point to a consistent pattern: most consumer health apps optimize for the platform layer (HealthKit/Health Connect) for the "easy 80%" of devices and then build a small number of deep, hand-maintained vendor integrations for their most differentiated data sources — the same "80/20" logic that led Health Connect to reach roughly 500 integrated applications within about a year and a half of general availability, while still lacking full parity with vendor-native experiences for details like ECG or proprietary running dynamics [web:16][web:36]. Health Connect's public timeline of vendor onboarding — Fitbit, Samsung Health, Peloton, MyFitnessPal, Oura, and Whoop at beta launch, with Garmin arriving nearly three years later and even then only as a partial, one-way integration excluding several metrics [web:36][web:55][web:61] — illustrates the market-wide gap BADAN should plan around: platform mediation is reliable but never fully current, and vendor coverage arrives unevenly and slowly. This creates a clear opportunity for BADAN to differentiate not by chasing every vendor API on day one, but by building the most honest, most complete provenance and reconciliation layer in the category — a place where the same measurement from two disagreeing sources is shown transparently rather than silently merged, which appears to be an underserved need across the mainstream competitive landscape.

## 9. Platform capability analysis: iOS and Apple ecosystem

**HealthKit as the default ingestion layer.** BADAN should read the large majority of Apple-ecosystem device data through HealthKit rather than attempting direct integration with every vendor, because HealthKit already normalizes workout and vitals data with source-application attribution built into its object model, and Apple bears the ongoing maintenance cost of vendor compatibility [web:34]. Where a device's vendor app already writes into HealthKit (the mainstream case for Apple Watch, Oura, WHOOP, and most scale/BP-cuff vendors with an iOS app), BADAN's job is simply to request the narrowest necessary set of read permissions per data type and to preserve HealthKit's own source-attribution metadata (which application wrote each sample) as the first link in BADAN's own provenance chain.

**Core Bluetooth and AccessorySetupKit for direct sensor pairing.** For BLE sensors that do not route through HealthKit at all — most cycling power meters, cadence sensors, and many chest straps used during a live workout — BADAN should pair directly using Core Bluetooth. As of iOS 18, Apple's AccessorySetupKit is the strongly preferred pairing mechanism over classic Core Bluetooth scanning, because it lets BADAN declare a specific accessory (by service UUID or company identifier) and present a system picker that surfaces only matching devices, rather than requesting broad "nearby devices" Bluetooth permission that exposes all discoverable devices around the user [web:34][web:35][web:38][web:42][web:43]. This directly serves BADAN's privacy-is-architectural law: the user grants a narrow, purpose-specific permission rather than a blanket one.

**Background execution limitations.** iOS constrains background BLE scanning and processing significantly; BADAN should not promise continuous background capture from BLE-only sensors (HR straps, power meters) and should instead frame these as foreground, session-based tools (live workout capture) while relying on HealthKit's own background-delivery mechanism for anything the vendor already syncs into Health.

**What BADAN should leave to Apple Health entirely:** ECG interpretation and any HealthKit type Apple explicitly reserves as a controlled or sensitive category should never be re-derived or reinterpreted by BADAN — BADAN reads and displays with full provenance, never re-diagnoses.

**When direct BLE is justified over HealthKit:** live, real-time display during an active session (a workout in progress) where HealthKit's own sync latency is unsuitable, and for sensor categories with no HealthKit-writing companion app at all (many bare BLE sensors have no companion app and write nowhere except directly to a pairing client).

**Handling data duplication and multiple devices:** when a user owns both an Apple Watch and a direct BLE HR strap used simultaneously, BADAN must never silently merge the two heart-rate streams; both must be retained with source labels, and BADAN's reconciliation engine (Section 16) determines which becomes the canonical displayed value while preserving both. The same discipline applies to multiple Apple Watches over time (device replacement) — BADAN should treat each physical device as a distinct `Device` record linked to a stable user identity, never conflating "my old watch" and "my new watch" data into an ambiguous single source.

## 10. Platform capability analysis: Android and Google ecosystem

**Health Connect as the default ingestion layer.** Health Connect is Android's on-device, encrypted health-data repository, exposing over 50 standardized record types across six categories — Activity, Body measurements, Cycle tracking, Nutrition, Sleep, and Vitals — and, as of March 2025, Medical Records in FHIR format [web:36]. BADAN should treat Health Connect exactly as it treats HealthKit: the default, lowest-maintenance path for any device whose vendor writes into it, with narrow per-type permission requests and preservation of Health Connect's own device/source metadata fields as the first link in BADAN's provenance chain [web:41].

**Known Health Connect gaps.** Health Connect explicitly does not carry ECG data or many vendor-proprietary metrics, and not every integrated app can read data written by other apps — some vendors only write, never allow cross-app reads [web:16]. Garmin's mid-2025 Health Connect integration is a useful concrete illustration of this gap in practice: it is one-way (Garmin to Health Connect only, no data flows back), and explicitly excludes running power, detailed cadence, and reportedly SpO2, body temperature, and HRV [web:55][web:61]. BADAN must therefore never assume Health Connect coverage is complete for any given vendor and should always offer the vendor's own OAuth API (where documented) as a Phase 2/3 supplement for power users who need the missing depth.

**Android BLE realities and manufacturer fragmentation.** Android's BLE stack is more fragmented across OEM skins than iOS's single implementation; background scanning limits, connection reliability, and permission dialog behavior can vary meaningfully by manufacturer (Samsung, Xiaomi, and others have historically imposed more aggressive background-process restrictions). BADAN's BLE layer should be built defensively — assuming background reconnection will be unreliable on some devices and always surfacing a clear foreground reconnect affordance rather than silently failing.

**Wear OS integration boundaries.** Wear OS data generally reaches BADAN through the paired phone's Health Connect instance rather than through any direct phone-to-watch BLE connection BADAN would manage itself — BADAN should not attempt to pair directly with a Wear OS watch as a BLE accessory; that relationship belongs to the OS and companion app.

**Samsung Health and other vendor ecosystems.** Samsung Health exposes a subset of its data through Health Connect, but some Samsung-specific metrics (certain BP/ECG features in supported regions) remain within the Samsung Health app ecosystem and may require separate, documented Samsung integration paths as a Phase 2 consideration [web:44].

**Google Fit legacy note.** Google Fit's REST API was slated to remain available only until mid-2025, with Health Connect established as the primary ongoing platform — BADAN should not build new integration against the legacy Google Fit API [web:36].

**Cross-platform coherence strategy.** BADAN's canonical data model (Section 13) must treat HealthKit and Health Connect as two parallel platform-mediated `DataSource` types feeding the same underlying `Measurement` schema, with platform-specific metadata (Apple's `HKSource`/`HKDevice` fields versus Health Connect's `Metadata` device fields) mapped into a shared provenance structure rather than maintained as two divergent schemas.

## 11. Bluetooth Low Energy and local accessory strategy

BADAN's direct-BLE architecture is built exclusively on public GATT standards and platform-documented pairing frameworks — no proprietary protocol reverse engineering, no bypass of vendor pairing security, ever.

**Scanning and pairing UX principles.** All BLE scanning must be explicitly user-initiated (a "scan for devices" action, never a silent background scan the user didn't request), and pairing must complete with a clear, named confirmation ("Connect to Polar H10?") rather than an ambiguous list of raw MAC addresses. On iOS, AccessorySetupKit's picker-based flow is the preferred mechanism specifically because it narrows the Bluetooth permission surface to the declared accessory type rather than exposing all nearby devices [web:34][web:42][web:43]. On Android, BADAN should request the narrowest applicable Bluetooth permission tier (scan vs. connect) per current Android permission model and should never request location permission merely as a side effect of BLE scanning beyond what the OS strictly requires.

**GATT service abstraction layer.** BADAN should implement one internal abstraction per standardized GATT service category (Heart Rate, Cycling Power, Cycling Speed and Cadence, Running Speed and Cadence, Weight Scale, Body Composition, Blood Pressure, Health Thermometer, Pulse Oximeter, Fitness Machine, Glucose/CGM services where a documented and lawful consumer path exists) so that any conforming device in that category is supported automatically without device-specific code, following the same logic that lets any conforming Cycling Power Service sensor work with any client that implements the standard [web:48][web:52]. Vendor-specific characteristics outside the standard service (custom vendor extensions) should be treated as optional enrichment only where a vendor publishes an official, documented SDK — never reverse-engineered.

**Reconnection, duplicate handling, and "device paired elsewhere."** BADAN must handle gracefully the common real-world case of a BLE sensor already connected to another app (a chest strap simultaneously claimed by a separate workout app) — detecting a failed or contested connection and surfacing "this sensor may be connected to another app" rather than a generic error. Historical-data pull from a BLE device is only attempted where the device's documented protocol explicitly supports stored-data retrieval (many simple sensors stream live only and retain no history); BADAN should never assume historical backfill is possible from a bare BLE sensor.

## 12. Vendor APIs, OAuth, SDKs, brokers, and import pathways

**Garmin Connect Health API.** Garmin's Health API is a documented REST-based platform providing steps, heart rate, sleep, respiration, body composition, stress, pulse-ox, and detailed epoch-level activity data via a ping/pull or push architecture, with an evaluation environment and backfill support during onboarding — but commercial use requires a license fee, which BADAN's business team must budget for before committing to this integration [web:56]. This is the correct route for power users who need Garmin depth beyond what Garmin's partial, one-way Health Connect integration provides [web:55][web:61].

**Dexcom Developer API.** Dexcom provides a formally documented developer program with sandbox data for initial testing, a tiered access model (Limited Access for up to five real-user testers, then a formal application for Full Access), and OAuth-based authentication [web:60]. Given the elevated sensitivity of glucose data, BADAN should treat any Dexcom integration as a Phase 3, legally-gated initiative requiring its own dedicated consent flow, never bundled with general device permissions, and with the same "never diagnostic" framing discipline applied throughout BADAN's other sensitive-data modules.

**Strava OAuth API.** Strava's documented OAuth API is well suited as a cross-vendor activity aggregator import path, but BADAN must preserve the underlying original device's provenance rather than treating Strava as the source of truth — Strava is a distribution layer, not a primary sensor.

**Samsung Health SDK/ecosystem.** Where Health Connect coverage is insufficient for Samsung-specific extended metrics, a documented Samsung Health integration path may be considered in Phase 2, contingent on Samsung's own partner terms [web:44].

**Brokers/aggregators.** Third-party health-data aggregators (services that normalize many vendor APIs behind one integration) can meaningfully reduce BADAN's long-tail integration burden, at the cost of an additional data-transit party and reduced raw-data richness (aggregators typically normalize rather than pass through fully raw samples). BADAN should evaluate specific aggregator vendors in a dedicated build-vs-buy pass before committing (Section 38), and any aggregator relationship must be disclosed transparently to users as a distinct provenance hop.

**Import pathways.** File import (FIT, GPX, TCX, CSV, JSON exports from vendor apps) and document import (PDF lab reports, clinical visit summaries) are durable, zero-vendor-dependency fallbacks that should ship in MVP regardless of how many direct integrations exist, because they are the only route for devices and services BADAN will never have a direct integration with.

## 13. Canonical accessory and device data model

The following entities constitute BADAN's canonical device-ecosystem schema. All entities follow "one data model, two altitudes": casual users see filtered, friendly projections of these same records; power users see the full entity with all fields.

**Device** — Purpose: represents a single physical accessory a user owns. Required fields: `deviceId` (UUID), `userId`, `manufacturerId` (FK to DeviceManufacturer), `modelId` (FK to DeviceModel), `nickname` (user-editable), `firstConnectedAt`, `connectionType` (enum: healthkit / health_connect / vendor_oauth / vendor_sdk / broker / ble_direct / fitness_machine / file_import / manual). Optional fields: `serialNumberHash` (hashed, never raw, for duplicate-detection only), `firmwareVersion`, `lastSeenAt`, `retiredAt`. Provenance/confidence: `connectionType` itself is the primary provenance signal. Privacy: moderate (device identity, not health values). Storage: local-first; only device metadata (never raw health samples) may sync to backend if the user opts into cross-device sync. Casual view: device name + icon + "last synced X ago." Power view: full record including hashed serial, firmware, connection history.

**DeviceModel** — Purpose: catalog of known device models for display (name, category, icon, supported GATT services/data types). Required fields: `modelId`, `manufacturerId`, `displayName`, `category` (enum matching Section 8 taxonomy), `supportedDataTypes` (array). Provenance: BADAN-maintained reference data, not user data. Storage: bundled/synced reference table, low privacy sensitivity.

**DeviceManufacturer** — Purpose: catalog of manufacturers for grouping/display. Required fields: `manufacturerId`, `displayName`. Same storage/privacy profile as DeviceModel.

**AccessoryConnection** — Purpose: represents the live or historical connection session between BADAN and a Device. Required fields: `connectionId`, `deviceId`, `connectionType`, `establishedAt`, `status` (enum: active / disconnected / revoked / failed), `permissionScope` (array of granted data types). Optional: `oauthTokenRef` (never the raw token itself — a reference to secure token storage), `lastError`. Provenance: this entity IS the provenance record for "how did BADAN get access." Privacy: moderate–high (reveals connection method). Storage: local-first; OAuth token material stored in platform secure storage (Keychain/Keystore), never in the general database.

**DeviceCapability** — Purpose: declares what data types/GATT services a specific connected Device instance actually exposes (may be a subset of its DeviceModel's theoretical capabilities). Required fields: `deviceId`, `capability` (enum matching GATT service or platform data type), `firstDetectedAt`. Storage: local-first, low sensitivity.

**DevicePermissionState** — Purpose: tracks per-data-type permission grants for platform-mediated (HealthKit/Health Connect) sources. Required fields: `deviceId` or `connectionId`, `dataType`, `state` (enum: granted / denied / not_determined / revoked), `lastCheckedAt`. This entity is essential for the "permissions revoked" edge case (Section 37). Storage: local-first, moderate sensitivity (reveals what health categories a user tracks).

**DevicePairingState** — Purpose: tracks BLE-specific pairing/bonding lifecycle distinct from general connection status. Required fields: `deviceId`, `pairingStatus` (enum: unpaired / pairing / bonded / pairing_failed), `lastPairedAt`. Storage: local-first, low-moderate sensitivity.

**DeviceFirmware** — Purpose: optional firmware/version metadata where legally exposed by the device/SDK. Required fields: `deviceId`, `firmwareVersion`, `detectedAt`. Storage: local-first, low sensitivity.

**DataSource** — Purpose: abstracts "where did this measurement ultimately originate," which may be a Device, a platform (HealthKit/Health Connect as mediator), a vendor API, or manual entry. Required fields: `dataSourceId`, `sourceType` (enum matching connectionType), `deviceId` (nullable — null for manual/platform-only sources), `sourceApplicationName` (e.g., "Oura app via HealthKit"). Provenance: this is the canonical provenance-chain root that every Measurement links to. Storage: local-first, moderate sensitivity.

**SourceApplication** — Purpose: for platform-mediated data, captures the specific companion app that wrote into HealthKit/Health Connect (distinct from the underlying physical Device), since HealthKit/Health Connect both expose a "source app" concept independently of device identity [web:41]. Required fields: `sourceApplicationId`, `bundleIdOrPackageName`, `displayName`. Storage: local-first, low-moderate sensitivity.

**Sensor** — Purpose: represents a specific sensing capability within a Device (a multi-sensor device like a smartwatch has several Sensors: optical HR, accelerometer, etc.). Required fields: `sensorId`, `deviceId`, `sensorType` (enum). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity.

**SensorStream** — Purpose: represents an ongoing or session-bounded flow of samples from a Sensor during a connection. Required fields: `streamId`, `sensorId`, `connectionId`, `startedAt`, `endedAt` (nullable while active). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity as metadata; the samples themselves carry the real sensitivity.

**SensorSample** — Purpose: a single raw reading from a SensorStream, before any normalization. Required fields: `sampleId`, `streamId`, `rawValue`, `rawUnit`, `deviceTimestamp`, `ingestionTimestamp`. Provenance: `deviceTimestamp` vs `ingestionTimestamp` distinction is essential for detecting clock drift (Section 37). Storage: local-first, sensitivity varies by underlying metric (HR = moderate, BP = high).

**Measurement** — Purpose: the canonical, normalized representation of a health value BADAN displays and analyzes, derived from one or more SensorSamples or from a platform-mediated read. Required fields: `measurementId`, `metricType` (canonical enum, e.g., `heart_rate_bpm`, `weight_kg`), `value`, `unit` (always normalized to BADAN's canonical unit, with original unit preserved), `timestamp`, `dataSourceId` (FK), `confidenceFlags` (array — see DataQualityFlag). Optional: `originalUnit`, `originalValue` (preserved for audit even after unit normalization). Provenance: full chain via `dataSourceId` → DataSource → Device/SourceApplication. Storage: local-first primary, encrypted backend sync optional per user preference; casual view shows normalized value only, power view shows original + normalized + full provenance chain.

**MeasurementBatch** — Purpose: groups Measurements ingested together in one sync operation, for efficient sync-session accounting and rollback. Required fields: `batchId`, `syncSessionId`, `measurementIds` (array), `ingestedAt`. Storage: local-first, low sensitivity as metadata.

**WorkoutDeviceBinding** — Purpose: links a logged workout/activity session to the specific Device(s) that contributed data to it (a run might have GPS from a phone, HR from a strap, and pace from a watch simultaneously). Required fields: `workoutId`, `deviceId`, `contributedMetrics` (array). Storage: local-first, moderate sensitivity (route data especially).

**DeviceSyncSession** — Purpose: represents one discrete sync operation with a Device/DataSource. Required fields: `syncSessionId`, `dataSourceId`, `startedAt`, `completedAt` (nullable), `status` (enum: success / partial / failed), `recordsIngested` (count). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity, essential for the sync-history UX (Section 18).

**SyncCursor** — Purpose: tracks the incremental-sync position per DataSource to avoid re-fetching already-ingested data. Required fields: `dataSourceId`, `cursorValue` (opaque, source-specific — a timestamp, page token, or API-specific cursor), `updatedAt`. Storage: local-first, low sensitivity, purely operational.

**ConnectionDiagnostic** — Purpose: captures troubleshooting-relevant events (connection failures, permission errors, timeout events) for a DataSource, feeding the troubleshooting UX (Section 35) and opt-in support diagnostics (Section 41). Required fields: `diagnosticId`, `dataSourceId`, `eventType`, `occurredAt`, `detail` (structured, no raw health values). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity by design (deliberately excludes health values).

**DataQualityFlag** — Purpose: attaches a machine- or rule-generated quality signal to a Measurement (e.g., "value outside physiologically plausible range," "gap-filled," "low-confidence estimate"). Required fields: `flagId`, `measurementId`, `flagType` (enum), `severity`, `explanation` (user-facing text). Storage: local-first, travels with the Measurement in exports.

**ReconciliationDecision** — Purpose: records how BADAN's reconciliation engine (Section 16) resolved a conflict between multiple sources for the same logical fact (e.g., "chosen: chest-strap HR over watch HR for this workout, per user's source-priority rule"). Required fields: `decisionId`, `conflictId` (FK to DataConflict), `chosenMeasurementId`, `discardedMeasurementIds` (array, but never deleted — only deprioritized), `ruleApplied` (reference to SourcePriorityRule), `decidedAt`. Storage: local-first, this is a core provenance/audit entity and must be exportable.

**DataConflict** — Purpose: represents a detected disagreement between two or more Measurements describing the same logical fact (same metric, overlapping time window). Required fields: `conflictId`, `measurementIds` (array, minimum two), `metricType`, `detectedAt`, `resolutionStatus` (enum: auto_resolved / user_resolved / unresolved). Storage: local-first, moderate sensitivity, core to the "honest interpretation" law.

**DerivedMeasurement** — Purpose: any Measurement BADAN computes rather than ingests directly (e.g., a weekly HRV average). Required fields: `derivedId`, `metricType`, `value`, `computedAt`, `inputMeasurementIds` (array — the derivation lineage), `formula` (human-readable description). Storage: local-first, must always be visibly distinguishable from device-sourced Measurements in the UI.

**UserDevicePreference** — Purpose: user-configurable preferences about a specific Device (display name, hide/show, notification settings). Required fields: `deviceId`, `userId`, `displayPreferences` (structured). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity.

**SourcePriorityRule** — Purpose: user-configurable or default rule determining which DataSource wins when a DataConflict occurs for a given metric type. Required fields: `ruleId`, `userId`, `metricType`, `priorityOrder` (ordered array of dataSourceIds or sourceType categories), `isUserOverride` (boolean — distinguishes BADAN defaults from explicit user choices). Storage: local-first, moderate sensitivity, core to Section 16.

**ImportJob** — Purpose: tracks a file or document import operation. Required fields: `importJobId`, `userId`, `fileType`, `startedAt`, `status`, `recordsExtracted` (count), `errors` (array). Storage: local-first, low sensitivity as metadata (the imported content itself carries the real sensitivity).

**ExportBundle** — Purpose: represents a generated export package. Required fields: `exportId`, `userId`, `scope` (all data / specific device / specific date range), `format` (enum: structured_json / csv / human_readable_pdf), `requestedAt`, `confirmationLevel` (enum matching BADAN's elevated-friction export pattern), `completedAt`. Storage: local-first metadata; the export file itself is generated on-demand and not retained server-side beyond a short, user-controlled window.

**DeviceAlert** — Purpose: user-facing notification about a device-ecosystem event (sync failure, permission revoked, low battery). Required fields: `alertId`, `deviceId` or `dataSourceId`, `alertType`, `createdAt`, `dismissedAt` (nullable), `severity`. Storage: local-first, low sensitivity.

**SupportDiagnosticBundle** — Purpose: an explicit, opt-in package of ConnectionDiagnostic and DeviceSyncSession records a user can generate and share with BADAN support, deliberately excluding raw health values. Required fields: `bundleId`, `userId`, `generatedAt`, `includedDiagnosticIds` (array), `userConsentedAt`. Storage: local-first until explicitly shared; requires explicit per-instance consent, never auto-generated or auto-transmitted.

**ConsentGrant** — Purpose: records explicit user consent for a specific connection, scope, or data-sharing action, distinct from platform-level permission dialogs (this is BADAN's own consent ledger, in addition to whatever HealthKit/Health Connect/OAuth consent already exists). Required fields: `consentId`, `userId`, `consentType`, `scope`, `grantedAt`, `revokedAt` (nullable). Storage: local-first, this is a legal/audit-critical entity and must be immutable once written (revocations are new records, not edits).

**AuditEvent** — Purpose: general-purpose, append-only log of consequential device-ecosystem actions (connection established, permission changed, export generated, conflict resolved) for both user-facing history and internal accountability. Required fields: `eventId`, `userId`, `eventType`, `occurredAt`, `relatedEntityIds` (array). Storage: local-first, append-only, exportable.

## 14. Data provenance, lineage, and quality architecture

Provenance in BADAN's device ecosystem is not a single field but a chain: every Measurement traces back through a DataSource, to either a Device (with manufacturer/model/connection type) or a SourceApplication (for platform-mediated data), with both a `deviceTimestamp` (when the sensor actually recorded the value) and an `ingestionTimestamp` (when BADAN received it) preserved distinctly — the gap between these two timestamps is itself a diagnostic signal for sync latency and clock-drift detection (Section 37). Data-quality flags are a first-class, user-visible construct rather than an internal implementation detail: a Measurement flagged "value outside physiologically plausible range" or "gap-filled by interpolation" must surface that flag in both casual and power views, calibrated in language (a casual user sees a small "estimated" badge; a power user sees the specific flag type and the rule that generated it). This lineage architecture is what allows BADAN to satisfy "provenance or it didn't happen" literally: no Measurement should ever exist in the canonical store without a resolvable DataSource, and no DerivedMeasurement should exist without a resolvable `inputMeasurementIds` lineage back to raw data.

## 15. Device identity, pairing, and lifecycle model

A Device record's lifecycle runs from first connection through active use, through possible replacement or retirement, without ever silently disappearing from history. **Onboarding:** a Device is created the moment a user completes a pairing or platform-connection flow; it is never speculatively created from passive detection. **Active use:** DeviceSyncSession and SensorStream records accumulate against the Device; DevicePermissionState tracks what the user has currently authorized. **Replacement:** when a user gets a new watch of the same or different model, BADAN must create a new Device record rather than overwriting the old one, and should offer (never force) a lightweight "is this a replacement for [old device]?" prompt purely for display grouping — the underlying historical data from the old Device remains intact and separately attributable regardless of the user's answer. **Multiple simultaneous devices:** a user with two active Apple Watches (e.g., one for swimming, one for daily wear) is fully supported — both remain independent Device records with independently tracked sync sessions. **Retirement/unpairing:** a user-initiated unpair sets `retiredAt` on the Device and revokes the associated AccessoryConnection, but historical Measurements already ingested are never deleted as a side effect of unpairing — deletion is a separate, explicit action (Section 33).

## 16. Source-priority and reconciliation engine

When the same logical fact arrives from multiple sources — Apple Watch heart rate versus a BLE chest strap, a Garmin workout versus the same workout re-appearing via Health Connect and again via Strava, a smart-scale weight versus a manually logged weight — BADAN's reconciliation engine must never silently merge or average the disagreement. Instead, it detects a DataConflict, applies a SourcePriorityRule (a sensible BADAN-provided default, always visible and always user-overridable) to select a canonical display value, and records the outcome as an immutable ReconciliationDecision — while preserving every original Measurement, discarded or not, in the underlying store and in exports.

**Default source-priority logic by scenario:**

| Conflict scenario | BADAN default priority | Rationale | User override available |
|---|---|---|---|
| Watch optical HR vs. chest-strap HR during a workout | Chest strap wins by default | Chest straps are widely understood to be more reliable during motion than optical wrist sensors | Yes — per-metric-type rule |
| Garmin workout vs. same workout imported via Health Connect/Strava | The richest/most detailed source (typically the vendor-native API) wins for canonical display; duplicates are flagged, not silently deleted | Avoids double-counting a single physical activity while preserving all richness | Yes |
| Oura sleep session vs. Apple Watch sleep session for the same night | Neither is presumed superior by default — both are shown side-by-side with an explicit "these differ" note until the user sets a preference | No universal "sleep sensor is always right" rule exists across device categories | Yes — user sets a default once |
| Smart-scale weight vs. manually logged weight, same day | Most-recent entry displayed as canonical for that day by default, both retained | Recency is a reasonable neutral default absent a stronger signal | Yes |
| Two different calorie estimates for the same session | Both shown, canonical display favors whichever source also provided the HR/power data used for that estimate, when determinable | Calorie algorithms vary too much in method to declare a universal winner | Yes |
| CGM platform data vs. manually entered glucose value | Both retained distinctly, never merged; CGM stream is the default canonical trend line, manual entries shown as discrete points | Manual entries are typically corrective spot-checks, not intended to replace the continuous stream | Yes |

**Deduplication signals** for detecting the "same workout from multiple sources" case rely on overlapping time windows, similar duration, and similar activity type — never on exact byte-for-byte matching, since different sources frequently round or estimate differently. **Merge-vs-preserve-separately rule:** BADAN merges only display precedence, never underlying data — the raw originals are always preserved and exportable. **User education:** any screen showing a reconciled value should offer a one-tap "why am I seeing this value?" explanation surfacing the ReconciliationDecision in plain language. **Downstream implications:** any AI-generated summary or analytics computation must operate on the canonical reconciled value by default but must be able to disclose, on request, that a conflict existed and how it was resolved — never presenting a reconciled value with false unanimity.

## 17. Device connection and onboarding experience

Onboarding must default to the lowest-friction platform-mediated route for the vast majority of users: a single "Connect Apple Health" or "Connect Health Connect" action that requests a sensible default bundle of data-type permissions with clear, specific explanations for each (never a vague "allow health access" blanket request). Direct-BLE and vendor-OAuth connections are offered as secondary, clearly-labeled "advanced connection" options reachable from the same entry point but never forced on a casual user who just wants their watch data to show up. Every connection flow ends with a first-sync progress indicator that sets honest expectations ("pulling in your last 30 days of data" rather than an indefinite spinner) and a plain-language summary of what was found ("connected — we found step, sleep, and heart-rate data going back 3 months").

## 18. Device hub and source-management experience

The Device Hub is BADAN's single home for every connected source, structured as a list of Device/DataSource cards, each showing connection type, last-sync time, and a status indicator (healthy / needs attention / disconnected). Tapping any card opens a Device Detail screen with sync history, data types being contributed, and (for power users) the full raw sample browser. A dedicated Source Priority Editor lets users inspect and override every SourcePriorityRule described in Section 16, always showing the current default and a clear "reset to BADAN default" option. The hub must never feel intimidating to a casual user — the default view shows only connected sources with a calm, uncluttered card layout; all diagnostic depth is one tap away, never displayed by default.

## 19. Casual-user device experience

A casual user's entire interaction with the device ecosystem should be: connect once, see data appear, occasionally get a gentle notification if something needs attention (permission revoked, sync stalled), and otherwise never think about "devices" as a distinct concept — their weight, sleep, and activity data should simply appear correctly attributed and correctly deduplicated in BADAN's normal health views, with device/source information available but not surfaced unless sought out.

## 20. Power-user device experience

A power user should be able to descend, from any displayed metric, into its full provenance chain, inspect every raw sample that contributed to a canonical value, review every ReconciliationDecision BADAN made, adjust SourcePriorityRules per metric type, browse complete DeviceSyncSession history with per-session record counts, and export any subset (by device, by date range, by metric type) as structured JSON or CSV. Nothing in the power-user experience should require different underlying data than the casual view — only a different lens.

## 21. Smartwatch ecosystem strategy

Smartwatches are BADAN's highest-reach category and should be supported first and primarily through platform mediation (HealthKit/Health Connect), since virtually every mainstream smartwatch vendor already writes into one or both platforms [web:36]. Direct vendor API integration (Garmin Connect Health API) should be reserved for Phase 2/3 power-user depth where platform data is confirmed incomplete, as with Garmin's own partial Health Connect coverage [web:55][web:61]. Huawei and Xiaomi ecosystems carry meaningful regional relevance (including for BADAN's Oman/GCC user base) but current platform-integration documentation is inconsistent enough to warrant a dedicated research spike before committing engineering resources — flagged explicitly as uncertain rather than assumed supported.

## 22. Smart-ring and recovery wearable strategy

Oura and WHOOP both have confirmed Health Connect integration [web:36], making platform mediation the correct MVP path for both. WHOOP additionally offers a documented, partner-gated API for power users wanting raw HRV/strain depth beyond platform-mediated summaries — appropriate as a Phase 3 initiative once user demand and partnership terms justify the integration cost. Newer entrants (Ultrahuman, RingConn, Samsung Galaxy Ring) should be treated as Phase 2/3 candidates pending verification of their current platform-integration maturity, which is evolving rapidly and should not be assumed stable without a fresh compatibility check at implementation time.

## 23. Fitness wearable strategy

The GPS sports-watch segment (Garmin, Polar, Suunto, COROS) represents committed-athlete users who value data completeness and raw-file richness over onboarding simplicity. BADAN's strategy here should combine platform mediation for baseline coverage with file import (FIT/GPX/TCX) as an immediately available, zero-integration-cost fallback for any workout data a platform doesn't fully carry — file import should ship in MVP specifically because it de-risks this entire category regardless of how vendor integration priorities evolve.

## 24. Heart-rate and endurance sensor strategy

Standards-based BLE sensors (HR straps, power meters, cadence/speed sensors) are BADAN's most engineering-efficient category: a single GATT-service abstraction layer (Section 11) supports the entire long tail of conforming hardware without vendor-specific code, and Apple's AccessorySetupKit further reduces the privacy and UX cost of pairing on iOS [web:34][web:42][web:43]. This category should be prioritized in MVP/Phase 1 precisely because the engineering cost-to-coverage ratio is unusually favorable compared to vendor-specific integrations.

## 25. Smart scale and body-composition strategy

Smart scales split cleanly between platform-mediated (vendor writes into HealthKit/Health Connect) and direct-BLE (Weight Scale Service / Body Composition Service) paths, and BADAN should support both, defaulting to platform mediation where available and falling back to direct BLE for bare scales with no companion-app platform write. Household-sharing misattribution — a shared scale producing readings for multiple family members — is a real UX challenge; BADAN's mitigation is a lightweight "who is this reading for?" prompt at connection time for scales explicitly marked as shared, never silent auto-attribution by weight-range guessing. All body-composition estimates (body fat %, muscle mass) must carry a persistent "estimated via bioelectrical impedance, affected by hydration" caveat, since this is a well-documented limitation of consumer bioimpedance scales, not a BADAN-specific concern to solve technically.

## 26. Blood pressure, temperature, and vital-sign accessory strategy

BP cuffs, thermometers, and pulse oximeters are BADAN's highest-privacy-sensitivity BLE category precisely because their outputs most resemble clinical measurements, which creates real risk of a user or observer misreading a consumer reading as diagnostic. Every reading from this category must display persistently alongside a non-diagnostic disclaimer and, where a reading falls outside a wide, clearly-labeled "you may want to mention this to your provider" band (never a clinical threshold presented as authoritative), BADAN should nudge toward provider contact using the same disciplined language patterns established for BADAN's pregnancy and period-tracking modules — "this may be worth mentioning to your provider," never a diagnosis or urgency grade. Direct BLE (Blood Pressure Service, Health Thermometer Service, Pulse Oximeter Service) and platform mediation are both viable; BADAN should support whichever the specific device offers, prioritizing platform mediation for lower onboarding friction.

## 27. CGM and glucose ecosystem strategy

Given the extraordinary sensitivity and regulatory weight of glucose data, BADAN's CGM/glucose strategy must be the most conservatively gated of any device category in this document. Dexcom's tiered, sandboxed developer program [web:60] is the most credible entry point but requires Full Access approval, its own dedicated legal review, and a consent flow entirely separate from general device permissions — this must not ship until BADAN's legal/product/clinical-advisor review gate (Section 45) has explicitly signed off. FreeStyle Libre/LibreView and other consumer glucose platforms carry the same requirement and, as of this research, their current documented developer-access terms should be independently re-verified at implementation time rather than assumed stable. All glucose data anywhere in BADAN must be framed as personal trend awareness, never treatment guidance, mirroring the non-diagnostic discipline established throughout BADAN's other sensitive-data modules.

## 28. Sleep-device strategy

Sleep tracking arrives from three overlapping sources — smartwatches, smart rings, and dedicated contactless sleep sensors (under-mattress or bedside devices) — each with materially different accuracy profiles that BADAN must never present as equivalent. Contactless sensors' accuracy limitations relative to worn devices are a known category caveat that should be disclosed rather than smoothed over when BADAN reconciles overlapping sleep data from multiple device types for the same night (Section 16's "shown side-by-side, no default winner" pattern applies directly here).

## 29. Fitness-machine and gym-equipment strategy

The Bluetooth SIG's Fitness Machine Service (FTMS) standard is the correct default integration target for gym equipment (treadmills, bikes, rowers), since it standardizes session metrics like speed, incline, power, and distance across conforming machines from many manufacturers without vendor-specific work. Machine-reported calorie estimates are widely understood to be imprecise and must always be labeled "machine-estimated" rather than presented with the same confidence as a wearable-derived estimate.

## 30. Environmental and emerging sensor strategy

Environmental sensors (air quality, humidity, ambient temperature) are a low-priority, contextual-overlay category rather than a core health-tracking priority for BADAN; where standardized BLE profiles exist, direct integration is technically straightforward, but this category should be deferred to Phase 3+ or later pending clearer user demand signal, and should never be built at the expense of core vitals/activity/recovery categories.

## 31. Device-specific data models and metric mapping

Metric mapping must convert every incoming unit and vendor-specific field name into BADAN's canonical `metricType` and `unit` vocabulary (Section 13's Measurement entity) while always preserving the original value and unit for audit. A representative mapping excerpt:

| Canonical metricType | Canonical unit | HealthKit source type (example) | Health Connect source type (example) | BLE GATT source (example) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| heart_rate_bpm | bpm | HKQuantityTypeIdentifierHeartRate | HeartRateRecord | Heart Rate Measurement characteristic |
| weight_kg | kg | HKQuantityTypeIdentifierBodyMass | WeightRecord | Weight Scale Measurement characteristic |
| body_fat_percent | % | HKQuantityTypeIdentifierBodyFatPercentage | BodyFatRecord | Body Composition Measurement characteristic |
| blood_pressure_systolic_mmhg / diastolic_mmhg | mmHg | HKCorrelationTypeIdentifierBloodPressure | BloodPressureRecord | Blood Pressure Measurement characteristic |
| spo2_percent | % | HKQuantityTypeIdentifierOxygenSaturation | OxygenSaturationRecord | PLX Spot-Check/Continuous Measurement characteristic |

## 32. Background sync, connectivity, offline, and battery strategy

Platform-mediated sources (HealthKit/Health Connect) should use each platform's native background-delivery/observer mechanisms rather than BADAN polling on its own schedule, minimizing battery cost and respecting each OS's background-execution model. Direct-BLE sources should be explicitly framed to users as foreground, session-based tools — BADAN should never claim or attempt silent background BLE capture, both because platform constraints make this unreliable and because unexpected background Bluetooth activity is a known source of user distrust. Offline behavior must be graceful: all locally-stored Measurements remain fully usable without connectivity, and any pending vendor-OAuth sync simply queues and resumes when connectivity returns, never silently dropping data.

## 33. Permissions, consent, privacy, and safety model

Device-ecosystem permissions must be requested per-purpose and per-data-type, never as a single "allow all health access" bundle, mirroring Apple's and Google's own platform-level per-type permission models [web:36]. Every DataSource in Section 13 carries its own ConsentGrant, distinct from the underlying platform-level permission, so that BADAN maintains its own audit trail even though the OS is the ultimate permission enforcer. Deletion of a device connection must offer an explicit choice — "just disconnect" versus "disconnect and delete all historical data from this device" — since these are meaningfully different actions with different consequences, and BADAN must never conflate them. No third-party analytics, advertising, or SDK embedded in BADAN should ever receive raw device-ecosystem data; this must be a hard architectural guardrail, not a policy statement alone (Section 41 covers this in build/vendor-selection terms).

## 34. Reliability, data quality, and conflict-resolution system

Reliability engineering for this module centers on graceful degradation: a failed sync should never silently produce gaps that look identical to "no data" — BADAN should distinguish visually and semantically between "no device connected for this period," "device connected but no data recorded," and "sync failed, retry available." DataQualityFlag (Section 13) is the mechanism for surfacing any value that fails a plausibility check (e.g., a implausible instantaneous heart-rate spike) without silently dropping or silently "correcting" it — flagged, retained, and explained.

## 35. Notifications, sync status, and troubleshooting UX

Device-related notifications should be sparse and purposeful: a permission-revocation notice, a prolonged sync failure, or a firmware-incompatibility warning — never routine "sync completed" noise. A dedicated in-app Troubleshooting flow, reachable from any Device Detail screen, should walk through the most common failure classes (Bluetooth off, permission revoked, app not running, vendor account expired) with device-class-specific guidance, and should offer the opt-in SupportDiagnosticBundle (Section 13) as a last resort rather than a first step.

## 36. Cross-platform parity and Arabic/RTL considerations

Device names, manufacturer names, units, sync-status strings, and timestamps must all pass through BADAN's localization layer rather than being hardcoded from vendor SDKs, since many vendor SDKs return English-only strings. RTL layout must be validated specifically for device cards (icon/status/action alignment mirrors correctly), sync-history timelines (chronological direction must remain intuitive in RTL), and any raw-sample data tables in the power-user view (column order and numeric alignment). Arabic terminology for device categories should be developed with a bilingual glossary reviewed by a native-Arabic-speaking team member rather than machine-translated, given the technical specificity of terms like "cadence sensor" or "pulse oximeter."

## 37. Edge cases and failure-mode catalog

| Edge case | Description | BADAN's required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Two devices claim the same physical sensor | A BLE HR strap already connected to a different app | Detect connection failure, surface "may be connected elsewhere," never fail silently |
| Device replaced mid-history | User gets a new watch of the same model | Create new Device record; offer optional, non-forced "replacement of X" linking for display grouping only |
| Vendor deprecates or changes API | Garmin/Dexcom/etc. change terms or endpoints | SyncCursor and DeviceSyncSession must degrade to "sync unavailable" state with clear user messaging, never silent data loss |
| Household-shared scale/cuff | Multiple family members use one BLE device | Explicit "who is this for?" prompt at connection and per-use if ambiguous, never silent auto-attribution |
| Clock drift between device and phone | Device timestamp diverges from phone's ingestion timestamp | Preserve both `deviceTimestamp` and `ingestionTimestamp` distinctly; flag large discrepancies as a DataQualityFlag |
| Permission silently revoked at OS level | User revokes HealthKit/Health Connect permission outside BADAN | DevicePermissionState detects on next check; DeviceAlert notifies user; historical data remains, new sync pauses |
| Duplicate workout from three sources simultaneously | Garmin native, Health Connect, and Strava all report the same run | Deduplication via time/duration/type overlap heuristics; richest source wins display, all preserved and exportable |
| Device with no companion app and no platform write | A bare BLE sensor with zero cloud presence | Direct BLE is the only viable path; if unsupported, transparently state "not supported yet" rather than implying support |
| Vendor requires a paid license for API access | Garmin Connect Health API commercial licensing [web:56] | Business decision required before Phase 2/3 build; document explicitly as a dependency, not a silent cost absorbed later |
| User exports data mid-sync | Export requested while a DeviceSyncSession is still in progress | Export reflects a clearly labeled point-in-time snapshot; in-progress sync noted, never presented as complete when it isn't |
| Subscription lapses | User's BADAN subscription (if any paywalled tier exists) lapses | All previously ingested device data and export capability remain fully available per "the repository is sacred" — only forward-looking premium sync features may pause |
| Device firmware update changes data format | A vendor firmware update alters characteristic format | GATT-standard services are versioned and stable by design; vendor-proprietary extensions are the primary risk, isolated behind an "enrichment, not core" architectural boundary |

## 38. Build-vs-buy and vendor-relationship considerations

BADAN should build its own GATT-service abstraction layer and platform (HealthKit/Health Connect) integration in-house, since these are foundational, long-lived, and central to the "provenance or it didn't happen" law. Vendor-specific deep integrations (Garmin Connect Health API, Dexcom API) and third-party aggregators are more appropriately evaluated case-by-case against licensing cost, maintenance burden, and user-demand signal — a dedicated build-vs-buy memo should precede any Phase 2/3 vendor-API commitment, explicitly weighing commercial licensing terms like Garmin's [web:56] and Dexcom's tiered access process [web:60] against expected user value.

## 39. Regulatory, legal, and compliance considerations

Glucose (CGM), blood pressure, SpO2, and any clinical-records-adjacent data (HealthKit Clinical Records, Health Connect Medical Records/FHIR [web:36]) carry materially higher regulatory exposure than activity/sleep/weight data across most jurisdictions, and BADAN's legal team must review data-handling obligations specific to each category before any such integration ships — this document identifies the categories requiring review but does not attempt to resolve jurisdiction-specific legal questions, which are explicitly out of scope for a product-research document. Regional considerations for BADAN's Oman/GCC user base should be flagged for dedicated legal review rather than assumed equivalent to US/EU norms.

## 40. Cost, engineering effort, and maintenance modeling

Platform-mediated integrations (HealthKit/Health Connect) carry the lowest ongoing maintenance cost since Apple/Google absorb vendor-compatibility work; direct-BLE integrations carry low-moderate cost concentrated in the initial GATT-abstraction build with minimal ongoing maintenance since standards are stable; vendor-OAuth API integrations carry the highest ongoing cost due to API versioning, licensing renewal, and vendor-relationship management, and should be reserved for cases with clearly justified user demand.

## 41. Privacy-by-design guardrails for third-party SDKs

No advertising SDK, analytics SDK, or any third-party code with network egress capability should ever have direct access to device-ecosystem tables (Device, Measurement, SensorSample, etc.) — this must be enforced at the architectural layer (a data-access boundary, not merely a policy document) so that even an accidental future SDK addition cannot silently gain access. Any vendor SDK BADAN does integrate (for a documented, approved vendor connection) must be scoped to the minimum functionality required and audited for its own data-egress behavior before adoption.

## 42. Known limitations and unresolved technical questions

This research explicitly flags the following as requiring follow-up verification at implementation time rather than treating them as settled: current Huawei/Xiaomi platform-integration documentation and regional availability (Section 8); current Ultrahuman, RingConn, and Samsung Galaxy Ring platform-integration maturity (Section 22); current FreeStyle Libre/LibreView and Lingo developer-access terms (Section 27); current Zwift and TrainingPeaks API/export terms (Section 8); and the exact current scope of Garmin's Health Connect data exclusions, since Garmin's own documentation was still evolving at the time of this research [web:55][web:61].
